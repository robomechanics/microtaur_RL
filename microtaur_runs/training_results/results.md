## Definitions

- **Return:** `Train/mean_reward`, the mean undiscounted return of the episodes that ended in that iteration. An episode lasts at most 1000 steps (20 s).
- **Final return:** the mean ± std of the raw return over the last 100 iterations (4400–4499).
- **T95:** the first iteration after which the 100-iteration trailing mean stays at or above 95 % of the final return for the rest of training.
- **T90:** the same as T95 with a 90 % threshold, as a sensitivity check.
- **Wall-clock:** the summed collection and learning time up to that iteration.

## Observations and caveats

- **Flat final returns:** all four variants end within 1.5 % of each other (107.8–109.4), and every episode runs the full 1000 steps. On flat ground the variants are separated by how fast they learn, not by their final return.
- **Flat convergence and the command curriculum:** the flat return is not stationary. It steps up when the first command stage changes at iteration 781, and it dips after the later stage changes. As a result, convergence times shorter than about 800 iterations can't be resolved, and the times depend on the threshold.
  - **Rigid, yaw and pitch** settle at about 820–850 iterations at 90 %. At 95 %, rigid and yaw settle at about 870 and pitch at 1186.
  - **Active roll** lags behind after the stage changes. It is below 95 % until iteration 2729 (1697 at 90 %) and only catches up after the last stage.
- **Rough warm start:** every variant reaches 90 % of its final return within about 100–130 iterations (7–8 M env steps).
  - **Roll and yaw T95:** these are late (1516 and 2780) because their returns keep creeping up slowly during the terrain curriculum, not because they fail to learn early. Their T90 values (102 and 126) show this.
  - **Rough return and terrain:** the rough return mixes locomotion quality with the terrain level each variant reaches (final terrain level: rigid 1.40, roll 1.06, pitch 1.33, yaw 1.21). A lower return can partly mean harder terrain.
  - **Active roll:** it has the lowest rough return and the shortest episodes (727 steps), so its episodes end early (terminations) more often.
- **Wall-clock is not comparable across machines:** flat and rough were trained on different PCs. Within the flat runs, rigid ran faster (~58 k versus ~47 k env steps/s; fewer joints, not verified). For comparing variants, report env steps.
